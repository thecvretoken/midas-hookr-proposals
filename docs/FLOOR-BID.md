# FloorBidHook

Prepared by Midas

Source: `src/FloorBidHook.sol`. Tests: `test/FloorBidHook.t.sol`.

## 1. What it does, and where the value sits at every stage

FloorBidHook gives an ordinary token launch a small balance sheet with no credit risk. Instead of burning a slice of every swap, which destroys the capital along with the supply, the hook accrues a quote-denominated fee and periodically parks it as single-sided range liquidity strictly below spot: a standing bid that the pool itself owns. If price falls into the band, the pool has bought its own token cheap and holds the inventory. If price later rises back through a band posted on the other side, the pool has sold that inventory back for quote. Either way the capital never leaves.

Since Nodar's question was custody and exit, here is the custody chain stated plainly, stage by stage. A buy pays 1.00%, of which 0.50% goes to LPs as the native dynamic fee and 0.50% is taken by the hook as a BeforeSwapDelta on the quote currency. That 0.50% is immediately minted as an ERC-6909 claim on the PoolManager in the hook's own name and counted in `_floorBucket`. It is never an ERC20 balance at the hook address; `test_buy_accruesExactFloorShare` asserts `_quote().balanceOf(address(hook)) == 0` alongside the claim balance, and `test_custody_everythingAccountedFor` asserts the same for both currencies after three full post cycles. When the bucket clears `MIN_POST` and the guards pass, `postFloor` opens a PoolManager unlock, computes a band, calls `modifyLiquidity` with the hook as owner and a per-posting salt, and settles the owed leg by burning exactly the claims it set aside. The value has moved from claims into a range position owned by the hook, and `test_postFloor_bandStrictlyBelowSpot_quoteIsOne` reads the position back out of the manager under the hook's own position key to confirm it is there. Rounding dust that the position could not absorb stays behind as claims and is counted back into the bucket, which is why the tests assert free claims equal the bucket exactly rather than assuming zero.

So at any instant every wei the hook has ever taken is in one of exactly two places: free ERC-6909 claims equal to the bucket figure, or inside a hook-owned range position. There is no third place, and there is no exit. The hook has no owner, no claim function, no withdraw function, no sweep, no rescue and no royalty. `test_noWithdrawPath_existsOrWorks` makes that concrete rather than rhetorical: it posts a floor, leaves a live bucket, then fires eight raw calls at the hook, `claimDeployer(bytes32,address)`, `claimRoyalty(address)`, `withdraw(address,uint256)`, `sweep(address)`, `rescue(address,address,uint256)`, `transferOwnership(address)`, `setFee(uint24)`, and an empty calldata call to catch a `receive` or `fallback`, from both the pool deployer and a stranger, sixteen calls in all, and asserts every one of them fails. It then checks the second escape route, the claims themselves: the hook has never set an operator and never granted an allowance, so `manager.isOperator` is false for both addresses and the allowance is zero. The only privilege anyone holds over this contract is the one-shot `setQuoteSide`, which declares which leg is the quote asset and cannot move a wei.

Exit logic is the other half of the answer, and it is a price mechanism rather than a permission. The band is posted strictly below spot and never contains the current tick, so the pool never buys at or above the price at posting; `_band` takes the smallest spacing multiple strictly on the far side of the tick and walks a full band width away from it. Price falling through the band is the exit: quote inside the hook's own position converts into the launched token, in place, without any call to the hook at all. `test_floorExecutes_poolBuysOwnToken` sells through the band and asserts the position now holds token and zero quote, that the liquidity is still owned by the hook in the manager, and that no token appeared as a free claim or as an ERC20 balance anywhere. `rollFloor` then flips a fully converted position to the other side of spot: a crossed bid is removed and re-posted as an ask a band above spot holding the token, and a crossed ask is removed and re-posted as a bid below spot holding the quote it realised. `test_rollFloor_repostsAsAsk_inventoryStaysInHook` and `test_rollFloor_askBackToBid` walk the full bid to ask to bid cycle. Inventory only ever becomes pool liquidity again; the roll path takes removal proceeds as claims and immediately re-posts them, and the leftover in each currency is asserted equal to the corresponding bucket, with the token bucket bounded at under one part per million of the ask.

## 2. Hook permission flags

`getHookPermissions()` returns `beforeInitialize`, `beforeSwap` and `beforeSwapReturnDelta` set; every other flag false. In `Hooks.*_FLAG` terms that is `BEFORE_INITIALIZE_FLAG | BEFORE_SWAP_FLAG | BEFORE_SWAP_RETURNS_DELTA_FLAG`, which is `(1 << 13) | (1 << 7) | (1 << 3)` = `0x2000 | 0x0080 | 0x0008` = **0x2088**. The test file mines the deployment address with exactly this constant in `setUp()`, and it is the same mask the deployed MidasRWAHook uses. `beforeRemoveLiquidity` is deliberately false: the hook never blocks an LP exit. No `afterSwap` permission is needed because the floor share is only ever taken on the quote leg, and when the quote is the specified currency the amount is already known in `beforeSwap`.

## 3. Authority surface

| Function | Who may call | What it can move | What it cannot move |
|---|---|---|---|
| `setQuoteSide(key, quoteIsZero)` | The pool deployer, meaning the address that called `initialize`, once only | Records which leg is the quote asset | No value of any kind; reverts with `QuoteAlreadySet` on any second call, `NotDeployer` for anyone else |
| `postFloor(key)` | Anyone | Converts the quote bucket into a hook-owned band strictly below spot | Cannot post above spot, more often than `FUNDING_INTERVAL`, below `MIN_POST`, or outside the reference band; the caller receives nothing |
| `rollFloor(key, index)` | Anyone | Removes a fully converted band and re-posts everything it held on the other side of spot | Reverts with `NotConverted` while the band is partial; cannot send proceeds anywhere but a new position or the buckets |
| `nudgeReference(key)` | Anyone | Moves the stored reference a quarter of the way toward spot | Cannot move it further than a quarter per interval; touches no value |
| `unlockCallback(data)` | PoolManager only | Executes the POST and ROLL branches inside the unlock | Reverts with `OnlyPoolManager` for any other caller |
| `beforeInitialize` (via PoolManager) | Whoever initializes a dynamic-fee pool | Records the initializer as deployer | Reverts on static-fee pools and on re-initialization |
| `beforeSwap` (via PoolManager) | PoolManager | Mints the floor share as claims on the quote leg, returns the fee override | Reverts with `QuoteNotSet` before the side is declared; takes nothing on the token leg |

There is no setter for `TOTAL_FEE`, `LP_SHARE`, `FLOOR_SHARE`, `MAX_TOTAL_FEE`, `FUNDING_INTERVAL`, `BAND_TICKS`, `MIN_POST`, `MAX_REF_DEVIATION_BPS` or `REF_SMOOTHING`, and there is no owner, no deployer share, no royalty, no pause and no upgrade path. Three of the seven rows are permissionless keeper calls that pay the keeper nothing.

## 4. Parameters and ceilings

Compile-time constants, in pips (1,000,000 = 100%): `TOTAL_FEE = 10_000` (1.00%), `LP_SHARE = 5_000` (0.50%, used when the floor share is also taken), `FLOOR_SHARE = 5_000` (0.50%), and the ceiling `MAX_TOTAL_FEE = 10_000`. When the launched token is the specified currency the hook takes nothing and returns `TOTAL_FEE` as the LP override, so LPs receive the full 1.00% and a sell is never cheaper than a buy. The posting guards: `MIN_FUNDING_INTERVAL = 1 hours`, `MAX_FUNDING_INTERVAL = 7 days`, `MAX_BAND_TICKS = 10_000` (roughly a 2.7x price range), `MAX_REF_DEVIATION_BPS = 1_000` (10% of sqrt price, which is roughly 20% in price terms) against `BPS = 10_000`, and `REF_SMOOTHING = 4`, giving the update `ref = (3 * ref + spot) / 4`.

Constructor immutables and the checks applied to them: `FUNDING_INTERVAL` inside `[MIN_FUNDING_INTERVAL, MAX_FUNDING_INTERVAL]`; `BAND_TICKS` strictly positive and at most `MAX_BAND_TICKS`; `MIN_POST` strictly positive; and the share arithmetic and ceiling are re-asserted even though both are constants. `test_constructor_enforcesBounds` proves both ends are accepted (`1 hours, 600, 1` and `7 days, 10_000, 1`) and that `1 hours - 1`, `7 days + 1`, a zero band, a band of `10_001` and a zero minimum post each fail. The test suite deploys with `FUNDING_INTERVAL = 1 hours`, `BAND_TICKS = 600`, `MIN_POST = 1e15`, against a pool with tick spacing 60 and seed liquidity 1e21. The requested band width is rounded up to the pool's tick spacing at posting time, so with 600 over spacing 60 the posted band is exactly 600 ticks wide, which the tests assert on every posting.

## 6. Failure cases and open problems

`setQuoteSide` is the sharpest edge in the contract. v4's `initialize` carries no hookData, so the quote side cannot be declared in `beforeInitialize` and has to be a separate call. It is deployer-only and one-shot, which means two bad outcomes are permanent. If the deployer never calls it, every swap on the pool reverts and the pool is unusable forever; `test_swapReverts_beforeQuoteSideSet` proves both directions revert. If the deployer calls it with the wrong leg, the hook will take its 0.50% on the launched token instead of the quote for the life of the pool, post bands on the wrong side of spot, and there is no correction path, because `test_setQuoteSide_isOneShot` proves the second call reverts with both possible arguments. There is no owner to fix it and no migration.

The floor is not a price floor. It is a bid of bounded size, funded at 0.50% of quote volume and posted at most once per interval. A sell larger than the band consumes it and price continues down; the band simply becomes inventory. Nothing in the design guarantees a price level, and the word floor should be read as a standing bid, not a support commitment.

A standing bid under spot is a price magnet, and the three mitigations are dampeners rather than proofs. The band never contains the current tick, funding is slow, and the reference guard refuses to post while spot is more than 10% in sqrt terms away from the slow reference. But an attacker willing to hold a manipulated price across several intervals can nudge the reference toward it a quarter of the gap at a time, and the guard then permits the post. The cost of the attack is the cost of holding the price, which is exactly the tradeoff the NatSpec claims, and it is a cost argument, not an impossibility argument.

The reference guard also punishes honest volatility. After a genuine large move, posting and rolling are both blocked until enough nudges have moved the reference, which the NatSpec puts at four intervals for a 30% move. `test_refGuard_pumpBlocksPost_nudgeCatchesUp` shows a 15% sqrt pump taking two nudges plus the intervals between them before a post is allowed again, and both roll tests wrap `rollFloor` in a `try`/`catch` loop that nudges and warps until the roll goes through, because a 10% move is enough to block it. Fees keep accruing during that window but cannot be deployed, and inventory sitting in a crossed band cannot be flipped.

A partially converted band is stuck. `rollFloor` reverts with `NotConverted` whenever the current tick is inside the band, which `test_rollFloor_revertsWhilePartial` exercises both on an untouched band and after a roughly 0.6% move into it. A band that sits straddling spot for a long time holds a mix of both currencies and nothing can move it until price fully clears one side. That is intentional, since tearing down a half-crossed band realises a price the design never chose, but the consequence is idle capital with no override.

Dust is permanent by construction. Rounding leftovers stay in `_floorBucket` and `_tokenBucket` as claims, and the token bucket is only swept back into liquidity on a subsequent roll to an ask. If no such roll ever happens, that token dust sits as claims forever. The tests bound it (at most 1 part per million of the post, at most 6 wei across three post cycles, at most 2 wei between the bucket decrease and what the position holds) but do not eliminate it. The same property that makes custody airtight, no withdraw path of any kind, means anything stranded is stranded permanently, including value stranded by a bug.

The band placement edge is tested indirectly. `test_postFloor_revertsWhenBandCannotFit` proves `BandOutOfRange` through `previewBand` rather than through `postFloor`, and the test comment says why: at prices within one band of the usable tick range a single unit of quote buys the whole token supply, so fee accrual cannot be staged realistically. `previewBand` calls the same `_band` routine `postFloor` uses, and the test also asserts on a normal pool that the previewed band is exactly the one posted, but the revert path itself is not driven end to end through a real posting.

The reference is compared in sqrt space, which means the documented 10% deviation band is roughly 20% in price terms. The contract says so in a comment, and adopters sizing the guard should reason in sqrt space, not price space.

The hook's own positions earn LP fees, and those fees do not go to LPs. On a roll, whatever the removed position collected in each currency lands in that currency's bucket and is re-posted as part of the next band. This is consistent with capital never leaving the hook, but it does mean the hook competes with the pool's LPs for fee flow on the ranges it occupies.

Finally, `nudgeReference`, `postFloor` and `rollFloor` are all permissionless and all pay the caller nothing, so the mechanism depends on someone volunteering gas. If nobody calls them, fees accrue as claims indefinitely and no floor is ever posted. There is no keeper incentive in this contract.

## 8. Where it fits in the Hookr stack

FloorBidHook's posting guards are a direct descendant of the sweep rails in the deployed MidasRWAHook (stock-token quote, `0xC97C22C241EcD0B9fb5656307e47C8a674ee2088`, Robinhood Chain, verified, unaudited). That hook limits its sweep to one call per hour, gates it on a 10% reference band, and advances the reference with the same `ref = (3 * ref + spot) / 4` smoothing; it also enforces a 3% slippage floor on the sweep itself. FloorBidHook keeps the first three of those verbatim, as `FUNDING_INTERVAL`, `MAX_REF_DEVIATION_BPS` and `REF_SMOOTHING`, and does not need the fourth, because it never swaps: it adds liquidity on the far side of spot instead, so there is no execution price to protect. It shares MidasRWAHook's `0x2088` permission mask, its `last != 0` first-action guard style, and its practice of holding everything as ERC-6909 claims rather than as ERC20 balances at the hook.

The difference from both deployed base hooks is the destination. GoldStandardHook (PAXG quote, `0xA0E75Ca3470638AF11417e0EAC79a1f50129e0cC`, Robinhood Chain, verified, unaudited) and MidasRWAHook each accrue to a recipient and eventually pay it out. FloorBidHook accrues to nobody: the accrual has exactly one fate, which is to become liquidity in the pool it came from. That is why the authority table has no claim row and why section 1 spends its length on custody rather than on distribution. For a launch that wants a balance sheet instead of a burn, this is the block; for a launch that wants the supply retired instead, BurnAttributionHook is the counterpart, and the two are mutually exclusive on a single pool because both claim the same 0.50% hook leg.
