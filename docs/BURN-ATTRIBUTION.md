# BurnAttributionHook

Prepared by Midas

Source: `src/BurnAttributionHook.sol`. Tests: `test/BurnAttributionHook.t.sol`.

## 1. What it does

BurnAttributionHook is a Hookr block that turns an aggregate incinerator counter into a per-address loyalty curve. Hookr's Hook Blocks already log burn buys in aggregate; this block records which address funded each burn contribution, keeps a rolling-window total per address, and pays a slice of fees back to contributors in proportion to what they funded. Routing through the pool beats routing around it, and the largest contributors in a window get the most back, but the rebate is capped low enough that a wash trade can never pay.

The fee is a constant 1.00% split three ways: 0.50% to concentrated liquidity providers as the native v4 dynamic LP fee, 0.40% to burn, and 0.10% to the epoch rebate pool. The two hook legs are always taken in one currency, QUOTE, fixed at deployment. When QUOTE is the specified leg of the swap the hook takes its 0.50% in `beforeSwap`, where the amount is known up front; when QUOTE is the unspecified leg it takes it in `afterSwap` off the realised delta. Everything the hook takes is held as ERC-6909 claims on the PoolManager until `sweepBurn` pushes the burn bucket to the immutable sink or `claimRebate` pays a contributor.

Epochs are fixed windows of `WINDOW` seconds counted from the Unix epoch, not per-pool clocks. Attribution accrues into the epoch that is open when the swap lands. Once an epoch closes its rebate pool is frozen, and each contributor may claim once, receiving the smaller of their pro-rata share and 50% of the burn they funded. Whatever the cap does not pay, and whatever is never claimed before the claim window lapses, rolls into whichever epoch is open at the time. Rolled value only ever goes back to future traders.

What it deliberately does not do: it has no owner, no setter of any kind, no deployer share and no royalty. It never blocks liquidity removal. It does not swap the burn bucket into the launched token; converting quote into supply destruction is the incinerator's job, and this block only delivers quote to the sink. It reads no oracle.

## 2. Hook permission flags

`getHookPermissions()` returns `beforeInitialize`, `beforeSwap`, `afterSwap`, `beforeSwapReturnDelta` and `afterSwapReturnDelta` set; every other flag false. In `Hooks.*_FLAG` terms that is `BEFORE_INITIALIZE_FLAG | BEFORE_SWAP_FLAG | AFTER_SWAP_FLAG | BEFORE_SWAP_RETURNS_DELTA_FLAG | AFTER_SWAP_RETURNS_DELTA_FLAG`, which is `(1 << 13) | (1 << 7) | (1 << 6) | (1 << 3) | (1 << 2)` = `0x2000 | 0x0080 | 0x0040 | 0x0008 | 0x0004` = **0x20cc**. The test file mines the deployment address with exactly this constant in `setUp()`. Both delta flags are required because the hook fee is taken on whichever leg carries QUOTE, and which hook that is depends on the swap's direction and exactness.

## 3. Authority surface

| Function | Who may call | What it can move | What it cannot move |
|---|---|---|---|
| `sweepBurn(key)` | Anyone | Sends the whole accrued `burnBucket[id]` to `BURN_SINK` | The rebate pool; the ledger; the destination; the caller receives nothing |
| `claimRebate(key, epoch)` | Anyone, paid only to `msg.sender` | Pays the caller their capped share out of a closed epoch's frozen pool, once | Another address's claim; an open epoch; an expired epoch; a second claim |
| `rollover(key, epoch)` | Anyone | Moves an expired epoch's unclaimed remainder into the currently open epoch's pool | Anything out of the hook; the caller receives nothing |
| `unlockCallback(data)` | PoolManager only | Redeems QUOTE claims and forwards them to the recipient encoded by the internal `_payout` | Reverts with `OnlyPoolManager` for any other caller |
| `beforeInitialize` (via PoolManager) | Whoever initializes a dynamic-fee pool containing QUOTE | Records the pool's config and which side QUOTE sits on | Reverts on static-fee pools, on pools without QUOTE, and on re-initialization |
| `beforeSwap` (via PoolManager) | PoolManager | Mints the hook claim when QUOTE is specified, appends to the ledger, returns the `LP_SHARE` override and the BeforeSwapDelta | Cannot exceed `HOOK_SHARE` of the specified amount; cannot change the LP override |
| `afterSwap` (via PoolManager) | PoolManager | Mints the hook claim when QUOTE is unspecified, appends to the ledger, returns the positive int128 take | Cannot touch the specified leg; cannot reattribute |

There is no setter for `TOTAL_FEE`, `LP_SHARE`, `BURN_SHARE`, `REBATE_SHARE`, `REBATE_CAP_BPS`, `CLAIM_EPOCHS`, `QUOTE`, `BURN_SINK` or `WINDOW`; there is no owner, no policy holder, no pause, and no upgrade path. Every value-moving path in the contract is one of the three in the top rows of that table, and two of them pay an address the caller cannot choose.

## 4. Parameters and ceilings

Compile-time constants, in pips (1,000,000 = 100%): `TOTAL_FEE = 10_000` (1.00%), `LP_SHARE = 5_000` (0.50%), `BURN_SHARE = 4_000` (0.40%), `REBATE_SHARE = 1_000` (0.10%), and `HOOK_SHARE = BURN_SHARE + REBATE_SHARE = 5_000` (0.50%). The ceiling `MAX_TOTAL_FEE = 10_000` is the same 1.00%, so the constant total is also the hard maximum. `REBATE_CAP_BPS = 5_000` against `BPS = 10_000` is 50% of the trader's attributed burn, which is 50% of 0.40%, which is 0.20% of volume. `CLAIM_EPOCHS = 2`, so epoch `e` is claimable exactly while `e < currentEpoch() <= e + 2`.

The epoch length is a constructor immutable bounded by `MIN_WINDOW = 1 days` and `MAX_WINDOW = 30 days`, with `DEFAULT_WINDOW = 7 days` exposed but not forced. The constructor also re-asserts the arithmetic it depends on: that the three shares sum to `TOTAL_FEE`, that `TOTAL_FEE <= MAX_TOTAL_FEE`, that `REBATE_CAP_BPS < BPS`, that `BURN_SHARE * REBATE_CAP_BPS < TOTAL_FEE * 2 * BPS` (the wash-safety inequality, checked at deployment rather than assumed), and that the sink is not the zero address. `test_constructorBounds` proves the window bounds are inclusive at both ends and that `1 days - 1`, `30 days + 1` and a zero sink each revert with the exact reason string. The test suite deploys with `QUOTE = currency0`, `BURN_SINK = 0x000000000000000000000000000000000000dEaD` and `WINDOW = 7 days`.

## 5. Accounting invariants and the tests that prove them

This is the section Nodar asked for, so it is worth stating the shape of the argument before the table. The hook holds exactly one kind of asset, QUOTE claims on the PoolManager, and it owes that balance to exactly two places: the burn bucket, and the sum of every epoch's outstanding rebate. Every invariant below is either a statement that those two ledgers add up to what the hook holds, or a statement that a specific path cannot move value between them in the wrong direction. The tests assert the strong form, equality to the wei, wherever the arithmetic permits it, and state the exact tolerance where it does not.

Conservation is the base invariant. The sum of `attributed[epoch][trader]` over all traders equals `attributedTotal[epoch]`, and that equals `burnBucket` when nothing has been swept, and the hook's held claims equal `burnBucket + rebatePool[epoch]`. `test_conservation_threeTraders` proves this across four swaps in both directions and both exactness modes, and also asserts `entriesOf(key) == 4`, one ledger entry per fee-bearing swap. `testFuzz_conservation` proves the same three equalities over fuzzed sizes from 1 wei to 1e21 and fuzzed directions, with the helper `_hookHeld()` reading the actual ERC-6909 balance rather than the hook's own bookkeeping. If conservation failed, the ledger would be claiming attribution the hook was never paid for, and the pro-rata denominator in every rebate calculation would be wrong in a way that lets one trader's claim eat another's.

Ledger monotonicity is the property that makes attribution meaningful as history. `attributed` and `attributedTotal` are written only by `+=` inside `_accrue`, and `entries` only by `+= 1` in the same place. `test_ledgerMonotonic` establishes a baseline, adds two more swaps, asserts each figure strictly grew, then runs all three value-moving paths (`sweepBurn`, `claimRebate`, `rollover`) and asserts every ledger figure is byte-identical afterwards, including the other trader's untouched share. If this failed, a claim could rewrite the denominator it was measured against, and a second claimant would be paid out of a total that no longer describes what was funded.

Rebate solvency is the property that the hook can always pay what it says it will. `test_rebateSolvency_threeClaimants` walks three contributors through their claims one at a time and, after every single claim, asserts both that the running total paid never exceeds the epoch's frozen pool and that the hook's held claims are still greater than or equal to everything it owes across all live epochs. It then asserts equality of held and owed once all three have claimed, checks that a repeat claim reverts with `AlreadyClaimed` and a non-contributor reverts with `NothingToClaim`, and bounds the undistributed residue at 3 wei, one per claimant, which is the floor-division remainder and nothing else. If solvency failed, the last claimant in an epoch would find the hook unable to settle and the transaction would revert on the PoolManager, which is the failure mode where a rebate scheme becomes a first-come-first-served race.

The rebate cap is what makes the whole design safe rather than merely balanced. Because unclaimed pools roll forward, a later epoch's pool can be arbitrarily large relative to the burn funded in it, and a pro-rata share alone would then hand a small contributor a large payout. `test_rebateCap_remainderRollsForward` constructs exactly that: a 1000e18 swap by one trader funds a pool that is never claimed, it rolls in full into a later epoch, and a second trader who funds only 1e18 in that epoch would otherwise take the whole thing. The test asserts `claimableRebate` reports the cap before the claim, that the claim pays exactly the cap, that the payment is no more than 50% of the trader's attributed burn, that the next epoch's pool grew by exactly the remainder, that the drained epoch reports zero outstanding, and that held still equals owed. `test_rebate_belowCap_paysFullShare` proves the cap does not bind in the ordinary case: a sole contributor's pro-rata share is 0.10% of volume, which is under the 0.20% cap, so it pays in full.

Wash resistance is the cap's consequence and is tested as an economic property, not an arithmetic one. `_roundTrip` buys with QUOTE, sells the entire proceeds back, waits for the epoch to close, and claims as the sole contributor, which is the maximum rebate the design can ever pay. `test_washNetsNegative` asserts the trader ends with less QUOTE than they started and that the loss is at least 0.80% of volume, which is the 0.50% hook leg paid on both sides, so 1.00%, less the 0.20% rebate cap. `testFuzz_washNetsNegative` asserts the strict loss over sizes from 1e12 to 1e21. If this failed, the rebate would be a subsidy for volume manufactured to farm it, and the burn counter would stop meaning anything.

Fee-split conservation is where the hook meets v4's own arithmetic, and it is the one place the document does not claim an exact 1.00%. `test_feeSplit_conserves` swaps 123,456,789e9 of QUOTE exact-input and asserts the trader was charged exactly the specified input, that the burn bucket is exactly 0.40%, that the rebate pool is exactly 0.10%, that the two sum to the hook fee to the wei, and that the hook's held claims equal the hook fee exactly. It then reconstructs the LP fee from `getFeeGrowthGlobals` and the pool's liquidity and asserts it within 1 wei of 0.50% of what reached the pool. `test_feeSplit_afterSwapPath_quoteOut` proves the same conservation on the `afterSwap` leg, and `test_feeSplit_exactOutputPaths` proves it on both exact-output directions while confirming the trader receives exactly the output they asked for.

| Invariant | Test | What would fail if broken |
|---|---|---|
| Sum of attributions equals the epoch total equals the burn bucket, and held claims equal burn plus rebate | `test_conservation_threeTraders`, `testFuzz_conservation` | The rebate denominator would not describe what was actually funded, and one trader's claim could consume another's |
| One ledger entry per fee-bearing swap | `test_conservation_threeTraders`, `test_ledgerMonotonic` | The entry counter would stop being a usable index of ledger history |
| Attribution and the entry counter only ever increase, and no value-moving path touches them | `test_ledgerMonotonic` | A claim could rewrite the total it was measured against, mispaying every later claimant |
| Paid never exceeds the epoch's frozen pool, and held is always at least owed, checked after every claim | `test_rebateSolvency_threeClaimants` | The last claimant of an epoch would revert on settlement, turning claims into a race |
| Undistributed residue after all claims is at most one wei per claimant | `test_rebateSolvency_threeClaimants` | Systematic rounding leakage would accumulate as permanently stranded value |
| Rebate is at most 50% of the trader's attributed burn, and the remainder rolls to the open epoch | `test_rebateCap_remainderRollsForward` | A small contributor could take a rolled-forward pool many times the burn they funded |
| Cap does not bind on an ordinary sole contributor | `test_rebate_belowCap_paysFullShare` | The advertised 0.10% share would silently under-pay honest traders |
| A round trip nets negative even at the maximum rebate | `test_washNetsNegative`, `testFuzz_washNetsNegative` | The rebate would subsidise manufactured volume and the burn figure would be noise |

| LP fee is 0.50% of what reaches the pool, within 1 wei | `test_feeSplit_conserves` | The advertised split would not match what LPs actually earn |
| Claims are one-shot, closed-epoch only, and lapse after `CLAIM_EPOCHS` | `test_claim_isOneShot`, `test_claim_openEpochReverts`, `test_claim_expiredReverts_andRolloverGates` | Double claims, claims against a still-moving denominator, or an epoch that can never be rolled |
| `rollover` is gated until the claim window lapses and drains exactly once | `test_claim_expiredReverts_andRolloverGates` | Value could be pulled out of an epoch while contributors could still claim it |
| Sweeping moves the whole burn bucket to the sink and leaves the rebate pool untouched; the keeper is paid nothing | `test_sweepBurn_sendsBucketToSink_leavesRebate` | The rebate pool could be drained through the burn path, or a keeper could extract value |
| Attribution follows hookData when present and the swap sender otherwise, never a third party | `test_attribution_goesToHookDataTrader` | A stranger could be credited, or credited attribution could be stolen |
| Epochs are fixed windows and roll exactly on the boundary; pools are independent per epoch | `test_epochs_areFixedWindows`, `test_multiEpoch_independentPools` | Attribution landing in the wrong epoch, or one epoch's claim draining another's pool |
| Rate constants are what the pitch says | `test_constants` | The document and the bytecode would disagree |
| Constructor bounds bite and are inclusive | `test_constructorBounds` | A deployment outside the documented window range, or a zero sink |
| Static-fee pools and pools without QUOTE are refused | `test_revertsOnStaticFeePool`, `test_revertsWhenPoolLacksQuote` | A pool the hook cannot denominate its fee in would open and accrue nothing |

## 6. Failure cases and open problems

The total is not exactly 1.00%. The hook's 0.50% is exact to the wei, but v4 levies the LP fee on the amount that actually reaches the pool, which is the input after the hook has already taken its share. The LP leg is therefore 0.50% of 99.50% of the input, which is 0.4975%, and the effective total is 0.9975%, not 1.00%. `test_feeSplit_conserves` reflects this honestly: it reconstructs the LP fee from fee growth and asserts it with `assertApproxEqAbs(..., 1)`, within 1 wei rather than as an exact figure, and it bounds the whole stack from above by 1.00% and from below by the 0.9975% floor rather than asserting a 1.00% sum. Any adopter quoting "1.00% total" to LPs is quoting the notional split, not the realised one.

Attribution is only as honest as the router. `_trader` decodes hookData when it is exactly 32 bytes and non-zero, and falls back to the swap `sender` otherwise. A router that does not forward the trader credits itself, which `test_attribution_goesToHookDataTrader` proves explicitly: the swap sender's own address gets zero and the router receives the whole entry. That is conservative in the sense that a stranger is never credited, but it is not neutral: a router that does forward can forward any address it likes, including one it controls, and the hook has no way to verify that the named address really originated the swap. The ledger is a record of what routers asserted, not of what wallets did.

The rebate cap forfeits value by design. When the cap binds, the difference between the pro-rata share and the cap does not stay with the trader and does not return to the epoch it came from; it rolls into whichever epoch is open at the moment of the claim. A trader who is also the dominant contributor in that open epoch will recapture some of it later, still capped. So the rollover destination is not neutral between traders, it favours whoever is trading at the time the roll happens.

Unclaimed rebates are forfeited on a two-epoch clock. A contributor who does not claim while `e < currentEpoch() <= e + 2` gets `EpochExpired` and their share rolls to future traders once anyone calls the permissionless `rollover`. Nothing is stuck, but nothing is recoverable either. With the tested seven-day window that is a fourteen-day deadline after an epoch closes.

Small swaps pay nothing and record nothing. A swap whose 0.50% floors to zero mints no claim, appends no ledger entry and increments no counter. The hook is silently free below that size.

"Burn" here means quote delivered to a sink, not supply destroyed. `sweepBurn` sends accrued QUOTE to `BURN_SINK`, which is `0x...dEaD` standalone and the incinerator inside Hookr's stack. Converting that quote into the launched token and destroying it is the incinerator's job. Deployed standalone against a dead address, the quote is simply gone and no supply is retired at all.

The monotonicity claim cannot be proved by a test, only checked. `test_ledgerMonotonic` proves the three known value-moving paths do not touch the ledger, and the test's own comment says the rest of the argument is by inspection of the contract: the only writes to `attributed` are the `+=` inside `_accrue`. A reviewer should confirm that by reading the source rather than by trusting the suite.

Epochs are global, not per-pool. `currentEpoch()` is `block.timestamp / WINDOW`, so every pool on a given hook deployment shares boundaries and a pool that opens mid-window has a short first epoch. There is no pruning of `attributed` or `attributedTotal`, so per-epoch storage grows without bound over the life of the hook, though nothing ever needs to iterate it.

The claim path costs a PoolManager unlock per call. Each `claimRebate` and each `sweepBurn` opens its own unlock and settles a single amount, so claiming is not batched across traders or epochs; the gas figures in the suite (for example 778,581 for the three-claimant solvency test) reflect that.

## 8. Where it fits in the Hookr stack

This is a block rather than a template, and the distinction is visible in the authority surface: there is no deployer share, no royalty and no recipient the author or the adopter can point anywhere. That is the deliberate difference from the two deployed base hooks. GoldStandardHook (PAXG quote, `0xA0E75Ca3470638AF11417e0EAC79a1f50129e0cC`) and MidasRWAHook (stock-token quote, `0xC97C22C241EcD0B9fb5656307e47C8a674ee2088`), both on Robinhood Chain, both verified, both unaudited, are complete term sheets that each carry a royalty leg. BurnAttributionHook carries none, because a block that plugs into someone else's launch should not be taking a cut of it.

What it does borrow from those two is the accrual machinery: the `beforeSwap` mint of ERC-6909 claims against a single quote currency, the permissionless push to an immutable destination, the dynamic-fee override returned from `beforeSwap`, and the `unlockCallback` settle-then-take pair. The extension over both is the `afterSwap` leg and the two return-delta flags, which is what lets the fee be denominated in QUOTE regardless of which side of the swap QUOTE happens to be on; MidasRWAHook takes its cut on the specified leg only. The `IBurnLedger` interface is deliberately separable: anything that can say "this address funded this much burn in this window" can implement it, and Hookr's aggregate burn logging is the natural first implementer alongside this hook.
